### Changed

- **A lane forms the batch that lands the most changes per verification, and a park for want of capacity counts against no change** (no route or event).
  - **The size.** It is the `n` in 1..queued with the largest `n(1 − p) / (1 + (1 − (1 − p)^n)·n)`, where p is the lane's recent rate of failures attributed to a member, as before. That is what a batch of `n` lands per verification when a failed batch of several re-runs each member alone; a batch of one lands `1 − p`. At p = 0.05, 0.1, 0.15 and 0.3 that is 5, 4, 3 and 3, where `ln 0.8 / ln(1 − p)` gave 4, 2, 1 and 1. With no failures it is the whole queue.
  - **What goes.** Core's `MAX_BATCH` (16) goes, as does the Candidate machine's bound of 16 members, and `batchSize(failureRate)` becomes `batchSize(failureRate, queued)` with `landedPerVerification(p, n)` beside it. A project's stored `maxBatchSize` still bounds its lanes; the default of 8 binds only below p ≈ 0.03.
  - **Capacity parks.** `BatchVerdict`, `SettledVerdict` and the Lane DO's `RecordVerdictCommand` take an optional `unrun: true`. KeelVerify sets it on a park that rests only on steps it could not run for want of capacity, whose every report failed `rate-limited`. `unavailable` still counts: a step no runner takes reports it too, and that is Keel's fault, not a busy box. The lane requeues such a park's members as `infra` without counting it toward `INFRA_FAILURE_LIMIT` or running any member alone. A composition MergeBox refuses `box-unavailable` is likewise held and never counted. A `timeout`, `schema` or `truncated` abstain may be the change's, and counts as before.
