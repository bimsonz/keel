# Changelog

Keel's contracts: the public API (`packages/spec`, emitted to `packages/contracts/openapi.json`), the MFP schema it imports, the ports in `packages/core/src/application/ports`, the DO RPC contracts in `apps/*/src/rpc`, and the runner protocol in `packages/runner-protocol`.

From `contracts-v0` on, every contract change gets a line here. Contract changes are additive (a new operation, an optional field, a new port or RPC method); a breaking change is listed under **Changed (breaking)** and says what a caller must do.

## Unreleased

### Changed (breaking)

- **Keel speaks MFP 0.2** (the domain's `mfp-wire.ts` and `memory/mfp.ts`, `why.ts` and the console's contract; ADR 0011). The spec imports `@bimsonz/mfp@0.2.1` in place of `0.1.0-pre.3`. The domain's model, its stored events, the DO rows and D1 are unchanged, so nothing migrates: conversion happens at the edge, through the domain's `premiseOfMfp`/`effectOfMfp` in and `premiseToMfp`/`effectToMfp` out.
  - **`declare`, `revise` and `rely`** take 0.2's per-predicate `MFP.PremiseProposition` (`premises`, `premisesAdded`, `premisesRetracted`, `premise`) and `MFP.EffectProposition` (`declaredEffects`): a bound's `value` is a number with a required `unit`, an authority's a list of writers, a shape a 0.2 type; a citation is `{kind: "message", ref: "<session>/<seq>"}`, a flat `{kind: "anchor", path, symbol?, commit?}`, `{kind: "record", ref}` or `{kind: "tool-result", ref}`. What passes the schema and does not read on its lattice (an ordering of a subject to itself, a cited tool result) is a 400 `request.invalid` naming the field, never repaired. An anchor, or an anchor citation, with no commit means the driver's working commit: on `revise` and `rely` the change's base, read from the change; on `declare`, where Keel has none until the workspace is granted, it is a 400 that says to name the commit or to add the premise with `rely` once declared (0.1 required the commit everywhere). A retracted premise is matched against the change's own by its 0.2 form, so a premise stored in an older spelling is retracted by echoing what Keel told.
  - **`Change`, `Preflight` and `Contradiction`** serve the same forms: `Change.premises` and `declaredEffects`, `Contradiction.premise` and `with` (either form). A premise's `strength` is always written, `must` where none was given. A stored proposition with no 0.2 form is left out and named in the envelope's `notices` (kind `PropositionUntellable`, ref the change or tenet holding it); the bare `GET /v1/changes/{id}` leaves it out with no notice. A move resting on a left-out contradiction is served with no `basis`, and one resting on a later contradiction with its index in the list as served.
  - **`WhyLink.record`** is a `KeelRecord {id, author: RecordAuthor, at, change?, evidenceClass?, scope?, message: MFP.Message}`: who stated it, when, for which change and on what evidence is Keel's record, and the message is MFP 0.2, naming Keel's profile `https://keel.town/mfp/profile/1` (`packages/spec/mfp/keel.profile.json`, prefix `town.keel`, which closes `verdict.outcome` to Keel's five outcomes and `observation.category` to `guard`). A decision's `chosen`, `rationale`, `alternatives` and `reversible` are the message's own members; a premise's proposition is `premise`; an intent has no `revision`; a guard's observation cites the premise it asserts and its probe; a landing is a `land` verdict whose reason cites the ruling (the landed commit is in its summary: 0.2 has no citation of a bare commit). A stored premise with no 0.2 form is not stated, and what cited it cites one record fewer. `MfpRecord`, `MfpAuthor` and the 0.1 `MFP.Evidence` go. `keeltown why` reads `record.message`.
  - **`EvidenceClass`** is Keel's one union for a verdict's reasons and a record's class (`VerdictEvidenceClass`, renamed). MFP 0.2 has no evidence: the console holds its own `Evidence`, `Cost` and `EvidenceSource`.
  - Core: the 0.2 schemas' optional members are exact keys (`Schema.optionalKey`), as JSON carries them; `MfpWriter` takes `KeelRecord`. Testing's `makeChangesMemory` leaves a declared change on its workspace's base, as the Change DO's `Start` does. `keeltown rely --premise` and `revise --add-premise`/`--retract-premise` take a value of any JSON type (an authority's writers, a shape), as 0.2 types it. The swarm's premise task declares its authority as a list of writers, typed by the SDK's premise. The app takes `@bimsonz/mfp@0.2.1` as a dev dependency, and `[why.mfp]` checks every message a chain states with MFP's own `checkMessages` under Keel's profile.
- **The Sketch leaves the domain and the event catalogue**. Nothing could open a sketch: `openSketch` was a stub from the start, the API had already lost `sketch` and `fromSketch`, and nothing outside the domain ever sent the Workspace a `Reassign`. So no stored event is of the five kinds below.
  - Events: `SketchOpened`, `SketchPromoted`, `SketchExpired`, `SketchDiscarded` and `WorkspaceReassigned` leave core's catalogue, `KeelEvent` in `events.tsp`, the console's reader and `events.md`. `IntentDeclared.sketchId?` goes; `WorkspaceGranted.holder` and `Workspace.holder?` are a `ChangeId`, and a grant naming anything else does not decode.
  - Core: the Sketch machine (`decideSketch`, `evolveSketch`, `replaySketch`, `sketchExpired`, `Sketch`, `SketchState`, `SketchCommand`, `SketchFacts`, `SketchEvent`, `SketchRejection`), the Workspace's `Reassign` command, `SketchNotFound`, `SketchStateRefused`, `Change.fromSketch`, `SketchId` with the id prefix `sk` (in `common.tsp` too) and the entity kind `sketch` (in `events.tsp` too). In the application: `openSketch` and its `SketchOpened`, `DeclareInput.fromSketch`, `sketchRepoName`; `declare` fails with `PoolExhausted | IdempotencyConflict`; `WorkspacePool.grant` and `grantWorkspace` take a `ChangeId`, and `WorkspacePool.holder`, `holderOf` and the pool RPC's `holder` answer one; a repo name is a workspace's (`<prj>-ws-<ulid>`) or nobody's, so `RepoOwner` has the one kind.
  - The Change DO's RPC: `DeclareCommand.sketchId?` and `SketchNotFound` among `declare`'s refusals go. A declare's fingerprint is unchanged (an absent field was never in it), so a retry across the deploy still matches its key.
  - Docs: the Sketch's section and chart in `state-machines.md` and the Workspace chart's reassign edge, the glossary's **Sketch** and ADR 0002's "and each Sketch".

- **A namespace's usage is counted under its own name: `OrgUsage` is `NamespaceUsage`**. The counts already followed the namespace (the port took a `NamespaceId` and every caller passed the project's namespace, a person's, an agent's or an org's); now the names say so. Nothing stored moves: every namespace's object keeps its address, `usage:<namespaceId>`, and its records and keys stay as they are.
  - Core, renamed: port `OrgUsage` → `NamespaceUsage` (`ports/namespace-usage.ts`) with each method's `org` argument → `namespace`; `usageOrg` → `usageNamespace` (`application/identity/namespace-usage.ts`); the Trunk's `orgOf` → `namespaceOf`. A `QuotaExceeded` title reads "The namespace is at its … limit".
  - Keel: Durable Object `OrgUsageDO` on binding `ORG_USAGE` → `NamespaceUsageDO` on `NAMESPACE_USAGE`, by wrangler migration `v4` (`renamed_classes`), which moves every object and its storage to the new class. **A deploy that holds `v4` cannot be rolled back past.** `OrgUsageRpc`/`OrgUsageCodecs` → `NamespaceUsageRpc`/`NamespaceUsageCodecs`.
  - Testing: `makeOrgUsageMemory` → `makeNamespaceUsageMemory`; the contract suite's harness hands out `namespace()` (was `org()`), and `freshNamespace` (was `freshOrg`) mints a person's, an agent's and an org's id in turn, so the contract runs over each kind. Tags `[orgusage.*]` → `[usage.*]`; mutants `c1-orgusage-*` → `c1-usage-*`.
- **What preflight suggests is no longer a move `resolve` takes**. The one union `Move` was both, so a request could name a move nothing makes.
  - Spec: `Suggestion` (`proceed`, `coordinate`, `refine`, `sequence`, `distinguish`) is what `Preflight.suggestedMove` and `PreflightIssued.suggestedMove` carry. `Move` (`escalate`, `distinguish`) is what `ResolveRequest.move`, `ConflictResolved.move` and a conflict's `move` carry. A `resolve` naming a suggestion is `400 request.invalid` from the schema, where every move but `escalate` was `503 service.not_implemented`.
  - Core: the domain's `Suggestion` and `Move` the same. `ConflictMoves`' `ResolveCommand.move` is `"escalate"`, the one move a Subject DO makes, and its refusal of any other move is gone.
  - `distinguish` needs `note`, the distinction: without one it is `400 request.invalid` on `body.note`.

- **The ruling on one question never resumes a change another question still holds**. A change with two conflicts before a person was resumed by the first ruling, and the adapter parked it again in the same commit; the rule was the adapter's. It is now the machine's.
  - Core: the Change's `Resume` command takes `awaitingRuling: EscalationId[]`, required: the escalations of the change's conflicts still with a person once the ruling is recorded, the fact `Batch` already takes in its blockers. While there is one the resume is refused `ChangeBlocked`, naming them in `escalations` with a `wait` first, after the state is checked; the change stays Parked. With none it is taken as before.
  - Core: `Park` is also taken from Parked awaiting-ruling when it names another escalation than the one the change waits on. `ChangeParked` then moves `Change.escalationId` and nothing else: where it was parked from, its lane, its plan and its lease stay. The same escalation is refused, by decide and by the fold. No event's shape changes.
  - The Change DO's reactions (in the same change): the resume carries `awaitedEscalations`; a resume refused as blocked is answered with a park under the first escalation it names, in place of the park that followed every resume; and an escalation heard while the change already waits for a ruling parks nothing.

- **The policy's resources name a namespace, and the kind is read from the id**. It follows namespaces with a kind: a field called `org` that could hold a person's namespace is gone from the policy.
  - Core, renamed: `AgentPrincipal.org` → `namespace`; `ProjectResource.org` → `namespace` and `orgSuspended` → `namespaceSuspended`; `OrgResource {_tag: "Org", org, kind}` → `NamespaceResource {_tag: "Namespace", namespace}` (no `kind`: `namespaceKind(id)` says it); `ForkResource.target` is a `NamespaceResource`; `ProjectNameTaken.org` → `namespace`. `Membership.org`, `OrgId`, the Org events and actions, the reason `org-suspended`, and `org` on the Agent record, its token and its enrolment stay.
  - Core, the table: `ResourceKind` gains `Namespace`. `Operator_listProjects`, `Operator_createProject`, `Namespaces_getNamespace` and `ManageCredentials` are decided on a `Namespace`; `ManageMembers`, `ManageOwners` and `DeleteOrg` stay on `Org`, which now means a namespace that must be an org (any other is answered as missing).
  - Core, the rules: `roleOf(user, namespace: NamespaceId)` counts a membership in an org alone; an agent's `Governs` counts in an org and in its own namespace, never in a person's or another agent's; a refusal for a missing role is worded by the namespace's kind (in a person's or an agent's own there is nobody to ask); the next-action titles of `org-suspended`, `private-source`, `foreign-project`, `inviter-left` and `inviter-suspended` say "namespace".
  - Events: `OrgCreated.personal` is removed (nothing wrote it since migration 0010). An event stored with it still reads.
- **An enrolment names its `namespace`**. The `Enrolments` port's `EnrolInput.namespace` replaces `org`, and so does core's `EnrolAgent.namespace`, now required. The AgentSession object's `EnrolCommand.namespace` replaces its optional `org` and is required: a command that names no namespace, or names it under the old field, does not decode, so a caller a version behind during a deploy has its call fail and enrols no agent nowhere. The enrolment's fingerprint is hashed as before, so one replayed across the rename is the same command. Nothing stored and nothing on the wire moves: `Agent.org`, `AgentEnrolled.org` and the entity kind `org` in an escalation's `affected[]` keep their names, each with a line in the catalogue saying it holds a namespace's id.
- **The refusal of a link an agent already has offers its end only to the agent** (for the console). The 409 `agent.state_refused` for an agent that already has a sponsor carried the next action `Agents_endSponsorship` for whoever asked, and a person who is not the sponsor cannot call it. It now carries that action, titled "End the link you have first", only when the agent itself asked (`askSponsor`); anyone else reads the same detail with `nextActions` empty. The sponsor never reads this refusal: their own approval is answered as the link they have.
- **An agent's public card names its namespace** (the card went live with the sponsor link's doorway, and its one reader is the console's `/sponsor` page, which moves in the same commit). `AgentCard.org?` is `AgentCard.namespace?`: the handle of the namespace the agent works in, which since namespaces have a kind is a person's own or an agent's own as often as an org's. `GET /v1/agents/{agentId}` answers `{id, name, kind, namespace?}`; nothing else of the card moves. It is where the agent works, as `AgentProfile.namespace` is; the agent's own namespace is its handle.
- **A missed sponsor approval says what to do** (for the console). `PUT /v1/agents/{agentId}/sponsor`'s 404 keeps its one body for a code that is wrong, used or run out and for an agent that does not exist, and its `detail` now tells the person to ask the agent for a new link; its next action's title says the same ("Ask the agent for a new link, and approve with it"). The status and `code` are as they were, so this is wording only.
- **Namespaces with a kind** (migration 0010). A namespace is a handle that owns projects: a person's own, an agent's own, or an org. Its id is its account's id, so there is no id space of its own, and a handle is unique across all three kinds by the `namespaces` table's own index. A person no longer has a personal org: their own namespace has no members, invitations or roles, and they own it by identity.
  - Spec: `NamespaceId` (a user's, an agent's or an org's id), `NamespaceKind` (`user`, `agent`, `org`) and `NamespaceRef {handle, kind}`. `ProjectView.namespace: NamespaceRef` replaces `org`; `CreateProjectRequest.namespace` (a handle: the caller's own, or an org the caller is a member of) replaces `org`. `GET /v1/orgs/{org}/projects/{name}` (`Orgs_getProject`) is now `GET /v1/namespaces/{handle}/projects/{name}` (`Namespaces_getProject`). `UserIdentity` gains `namespace?: NamespaceRef` and its `orgs` never lists the user's own namespace. `AgentProfile.org` is now `AgentProfile.namespace` (a view, and it holds a person's id as often as an org's); `AgentEnrolled.org` keeps its name, which the log already holds, and widens to `NamespaceId`. `ProjectView.viewer.role` is `owner` for a person in their own namespace.
  - An agent's token carries the namespace it is for as the claim `ns`, in place of `org`. A token minted before this is refused, and the agent exchanges its credential again.
  - Kernel: `NamespaceId`, `NamespaceKind`, `namespaceKind(id)`. Ports: `Namespaces` (`byId`, `byHandle`; `NamespaceRecord {namespace, handle, kind, suspended}`) replaces `Orgs`, and `ProjectNamespaces` (`ProjectPlace {projectId, namespace, name}`, `inNamespace`) replaces `ProjectOrgs`. `Memberships.standing(userId, namespace)` answers `owner` in the user's own namespace and no role in a namespace that is neither theirs nor an org. Every `namespace` and `org` parameter that named an org by `OrgId` takes a `NamespaceId`; `OrgId` is left for what is an org and nothing else. `AgentDirectory.upsert` never moves a placed agent's namespace.
  - Policy: `OrgResource` gains `kind`; a user holds `owner` in the namespace whose id is its own (`roleOf`); `ManageMembers`, `ManageOwners` and `DeleteOrg` asked of a namespace that is no org are NotVisible; `personOwns(kind)` is false only for an agent's own namespace. The field names `org` in `tenancy.ts` and `authorize.ts` are unchanged.
  - `createProject` takes `namespace`; its creator is a `UserId` (`CreateProject.by`, the Project DO's `CreateProjectCommand.by`) and its commit's actor is `user:<id>`, where it was `operator:<id>`.
  - Better Auth: no personal org is made, `PERSONAL_ORG` is gone, and Better Auth's organization routes answer a person's namespace as an org that does not exist. `NAME_TAKEN` is decided by the table: a sign-up or an org whose handle is taken in the same moment is refused by the statement that would have written it, and nothing is made.
  - Migration 0010 is not expand-only. It re-homes each project in a personal org, and that project's agents, to the person's own namespace, then deletes every personal org's `organization`, `member` and `invitation` rows and clears a session's active org that pointed at one. No project, agent, user or other org is deleted. Usage counted before 0010 under a personal org is not carried over, and nothing was open under a personal org's escalations.
  - A handle has one holder, whoever asks: an org by its maker's own handle is `NAME_TAKEN`, and so is choosing the handle one already has. A sign-up, a chosen handle or an org that loses a handle to another account in the same moment is `NAME_TAKEN` too, from the database's refusal (a passkey sign-up answered 500 there before this); the one exception is an org taking another org's slug when Better Auth's own read sees it first, which answers Better Auth's `ORGANIZATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`. An org's handle is checked before Better Auth reads slugs, so a reserved or taken one answers Keel's code. The reserved-name refusal reads `"<name>" is reserved`, naming no host.
  - Better Auth's member routes on a person's namespace answer as it finds them: `ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND` by handle or id, and "not a member" (`MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `YOU_ARE_NOT_A_MEMBER_OF_THIS_ORGANIZATION`, `USER_IS_NOT_A_MEMBER_OF_THE_ORGANIZATION`) to invite, list or make it active. Nothing is written.
  - Routing: `<host>/.well-known/*` goes to `keel`, one rule.
  - The agent card (`Agents_getAgent`) fills its `org` with the handle of the namespace the agent works in, a person's as often as an org's; the field keeps its name.
  - CLI: `keeltown project create --namespace <handle>` replaces `--org`. SDK: `client.namespace(handle)`; `client.project(handle, name)`.

- **Where a project starts**. `CreateProjectRequest.source` was a string, a template's name or a project's trunk repo; it is now one of four starts, told apart by `kind`: `{kind: "empty"}`, `{kind: "import", url, ref?}`, `{kind: "fork", project}` (the project's id) and `{kind: "template", name}`. `template` is an interim kind: it leaves the API once Keel's fixture is a public repository to import.
  - **Empty.** Keel writes trunk's first commit itself: a `README.md` naming the project, authored and committed by Keel at the time the create began, and signed as every commit Keel writes is. A retry writes the same commit.
  - **Import.** A public Git repository, imported once by the code host. The URL is `https://<host>/<path>` and nothing more (no credential, port, query or fragment, and a host by name); it is recorded as the project's origin for every reader. The upstream's default branch comes, or the one `ref` names by its short name; when it is not `main`, trunk's `main` is made at its head and the upstream branch stays beside it. Its whole history is admitted against the namespace's repository quota before the project exists.
  - **Refusals, each with a next action:** a URL or branch Keel takes none like is 400 `request.invalid` with an issue on `source.url` or `source.ref`; an import the code host took nothing from is `project.import_refused` (404 for a repository that is not there or not public, something that is not Git, or a missing branch; 503 when the upstream did not answer; 403 over the code host's own size limit); a history over what Keel copies in one piece is 403 `project.import_too_large`; one over what the namespace has left is 403 `quota.exceeded`. A refused start makes no project, keeps no repository and gives the name back.
  - Spec: `ProjectSource` (`EmptySource`, `ImportSource`, `ForkSource`, `TemplateSource`); problem codes `project.import_refused`, `project.import_too_large` and `quota.exceeded`.
  - Ports: `ProjectSource` (`Empty | Import | Project | Template`) and `ProjectStartRefused` on `Projects.create`, which takes the start where it took a repo's name; `ProjectNamespaces.release(projectId)` gives back the place of a project that was never made; `Trunks.initialize(projectId, head, by, origin)` fails `QuotaExceeded | ImportTooLarge` for an import and records the origin; `Trunks.adopt`, `Trunks.seed`, `Trunks.origin`; `CodeHost.import` (`ImportRefused`, five reasons) and `CodeHost.branches`; `TrunkWriter.adopt` and `seed`; `RELAY_PACK_CAP_BYTES`.
  - RPC: the Project DO's `CreateProjectCommand.source` is the start by `_tag`, and `createProject` answers `ImportRefused`, `QuotaExceeded` and `ImportTooLarge` beside `RepoNotFound`; the Trunk DO's `initialize(head, by, origin)`, `adopt(commit)`, `seed(name, at)`, and `origin` on its status.
  - `createProject` initializes trunk before it makes staging and the mirror, so a refused import leaves one repository to delete.
  - CLI: `keeltown project create` takes exactly one of `--empty`, `--import <url> [--ref <branch>]`, `--fork <prj_…>` and `--template <name>`, in place of `--source`.
- **A `why` link carries its MFP record** (the domain's `WhyLink`; core only, and nothing read it: `why` was a stub). The domain's `WhyLink {summary, recordId}` is `{summary, record: MfpRecord}`, as the spec's `WhyLink` always had it. The stub `why(principal, target)` is the use-case `why(project, target)`, and `WhyTarget`'s `{kind: "line", path, line}` is `{kind: "path", path, line?}`.

### Changed

- **A candidate with no vitest bounces at once, saying to add it** (the suite's runner protocol gains one marker). The js-ts suite runs vitest from the candidate's own dependencies, never its `test` script. A candidate without vitest used to abstain "no test report" with the same evidence every round, then park three times and bounce as an infra failure, with nothing to act on. The suite command now checks for `node_modules/vitest/vitest.mjs` once the deps are in place and prints `KEEL_VITEST ok` or `KEEL_VITEST none`; on `none` it drops the deps and ends. `parseSuiteOutput` reads `none` as its own tag, `no-vitest`, and a run without the marker as malformed. The judge fails it as the candidate's dependencies refused (`deps-refused: no-vitest`, with no failing tests), so the bounce cites `no-vitest` and what to do: add vitest as a devDependency, with at least one test, and commit the `pnpm-lock.yaml`. A run with vitest that writes no report still abstains.
- **MergeBox composes what GitCore refuses** (additive: one optional event field, no route or migration). The Lane composes in tiers: GitCore first (Tier 0), and a batch it refuses goes to MergeBox (Tier 2), a container per project under the `durable_object` scheduling policy (`MergeBox`, image `apps/keel/containers/mergebox/Dockerfile`: Debian trixie-slim pinned by digest, git 2.47), gated per namespace by BoxGate (`boxgate:<namespaceId>`, leases up to the namespace's `concurrent-containers`, reclaimed at their deadline). The box composes each member with git's `merge-tree` and pushes unsigned trees to a scratch ref; keel writes the commits over those trees as GitCore would, signed with the deployment's key, and the candidate lands as `composer: "mergebox"`, `tier: 2`, its `tierReason` naming GitCore's refusal. The box has no internet and no credential: its only way out is the Artifacts host, where the Worker's egress mints a ten-minute token for each request to a repo of the compose (a push to staging alone), and every other host is refused. After every compose the box is checked for any token, with a canary proving the check can see. A conflict git finds bounces as before, with the box's paths. A box that is busy, fails or fails its check is infra's: the verdict is `hold`, the change is requeued, and the third in a row of a batch of one bounces, told to submit again unchanged. A forming batch may now fail for infra. Probes compose with GitCore alone.
- **keel-verify's boxes run on one current image** (no contract change). The suite box and the deps box share `apps/keel-verify/containers/sandbox/Dockerfile`: Ubuntu 24.04, the base CI runs on, with the server of `@cloudflare/sandbox` 0.12.10 (the workspace's catalog pins it, and an override holds `@cloudflare/ci` to it, so one SDK drives both classes and matches the image); git 2.55.0, built from kernel.org's tarball checked by its sha256, so GitCore's oracle tests pass in a box as they do in CI; node 22.22.3 from nodejs.org's tarball checked by its sha256; pnpm 11.18.0; and `openssh-client`. The toolchain line names the base too (`DEPS_TOOLCHAIN` is `node v22.22.3 pnpm 11.18.0 ubuntu 24.04`), so a deps bundle built on the earlier image is treated as missing and built again once, under the same install digest.

Keel's Worker also sets `limits.subrequests` to 200,000: each read costs two subrequests, and under the default 10,000 every bundle of Keel's tree stopped at read 5,004.
- **MergeBox composes live** (no route, event or migration). The box registered its egress after its CA trust script, and the platform writes its CA only once an HTTPS intercept is registered, so the script timed out and every compose came back `box-failed`. The box is now readied as start, first command, egress, trust, plant, and a box it reuses has its egress registered again before each compose. A box failure's `BatchFailed` and `VerdictIssued` evidence now says what failed in the box (`composition-refused: box-failed: <what>`: the script and its exit, or the box's own answer), with remotes, 32-hex ids and token shapes taken out; core's `CompositionRefused` gains an optional `detail` for it.
- An agent's inbox answers a token that names no project (`GET /v1/inbox`, the MCP `inbox` tool and `keel://inbox`). It answered `500 internal.defect`, though the inbox is the agent's and holds its own notices, and `whoami` tells an agent in no org to wait there for its invitation. `keeltown whoami` run as a person reads the person's own record (`GET /v1/users/me`); it asked for an agent's, which Keel refuses a person.
- The token endpoint binds a token only to a key whose proofs Keel takes: a DPoP proof made with `EdDSA` or `ES256`, the two the authorization server's document names. It used to take the library's wider list, so a request with a proof made by a P-521 or RSA key was granted a token that every Keel door then refused. Such a request is now refused `400 invalid_dpop_proof`.

### Removed

- **`merge` and `yield` leave `Move`** (breaking). Neither was ever made or emitted. The domain's tenet machine no longer counts `yield` among the moves that leave a tenet standing.
- **The `SecretsProvider` port and `SecretMissing`** (breaking; nothing in an app used them). `CredentialStore` replaces them. `makeSecretsProviderMemory` is gone from `@keeltown/testing`, and keel's `DEPLOY_TOKEN` Secrets Store binding is replaced by `CREDENTIAL_KEK`.
- **`sketch` and a workspace's release leave the API** (breaking). Each has only ever answered `503 service.not_implemented`.
  - `POST /v1/sketches` (`sketch`) with `SketchRequest`, `SketchView` and `SketchEnvelope`: sketches are cut. Declare a change to get a workspace.
  - `DeclareRequest.fromSketch` and the problem code `sketch.not_found`: with no way to open a sketch the field could only answer 404. A declare that still sends it is refused `400 request.invalid`, as any field the contract lacks.
  - `POST /v1/workspaces/{workspaceId}/release` (`Workspaces_releaseWorkspace`) with `WorkspaceReleased`: a change has no state without its workspace. `abandon` ends the change and its workspace is collected.
  - The SDK's `sketch()` and `releaseWorkspace()`, the MCP tools of both, and the policy's rows for both go with them. The paths now answer `404 resource.not_found`.
  - Stays: the domain's Sketch machine, its events (`SketchOpened`, `SketchPromoted`, `SketchExpired`, `SketchDiscarded`, `WorkspaceReassigned`) and `SketchId` in the event catalogue; core's `DeclareIntent.fromSketch?` and the Change DO's RPC, which the HTTP adapter no longer fills.

- `Views_getCandidate` (`GET /v1/candidates/{candidateId}`) with `CandidateView`, and `TrunkView.redCount` (**breaking**). The route answered 503 and nothing called it: it names no project, nothing maps a candidate to one, and a project's events already carry a candidate's whole life with both ids (`BatchFormed`, `CandidateAssembled` with its tier, `VerificationStarted`, `VerdictIssued`, then `BatchFailed` or `CandidatePromoted`). `redCount` was a constant 0 that read as a measurement: nothing audits trunk after a landing, and the claim that trunk stays green rests on a candidate being verified before it is promoted. If an auditor is built the field comes back with a real number. With them go the SDK's `candidate()`, the policy table's `Views_getCandidate` row, core's `getCandidate` stub and its `TrunkView.redCount`, `LaneRpc.batch` and `LaneCodecs.batch` (declared, never implemented), and `redCount` from the Trunk object's `TrunkStatus`.
- `ProjectCreated.forkedFrom?`. Nothing wrote it: where a project started is recorded once, as `TrunkInitialized.origin`, and a project's view reads that.
- The scope checks the policy replaced (the cutover): `satisfies` and the generated routes' scope test, the MCP tool filter, `confineBootstrap`/`BOOTSTRAP_MAY_CALL`, read-scope's `readsInstanceWide`/`projectScope`/`readScope`/`viewScope` with `TokenRequired`/`ForeignProject`/`ReadRefused`, `repoAccess`, `withdrawAuthority`'s scope test, and the inbox streams' own checks.
- `scripts/deploy.ts` (tooling, no contract change): the console's block is gone. The console's Worker holds no credential, so the deploy no longer sets the observer's and operator's client credentials on it, nor generates `CONSOLE_CAPABILITY_KEY` and `CONSOLE_PRESENTER_KEY`, and `--rotate` is removed with them. The `console` entry of `.keel/<env>.json` is no longer read or written. `apps/console/wrangler.jsonc` loses its six `secrets.required` and its service binding to `keel`.
- `L1_SUITE_PLAN` in `@keeltown/core/application/verification`: nothing runs a static plan; tests compile theirs (`suitePlan`, `workerAppPlan`, `guardedPlan` and `TEST_GUARD` in `@keeltown/testing`).
- `SubjectRpc.rely` and its codec: a change relies on a subject through its own Change DO only (`ChangeRpc.rely`), which subscribes the subject.
- `LaneRpc.enqueue` and `LaneCodecs.enqueue`, which nothing called: a change joins its lane by its ChangeQueued only (no callers).
- No account is made for anyone (**breaking**):
  - `Admin_createUser` (`POST /v1/admin/users`) with `CreateUserRequest`, `CreatedUser`, `SessionGrant`, the `BootstrapAuth` scheme and the problem code `user.handle_refused`; the SDK's `createUser`;
  - the claim: the `Keel-Claim` header, `CLAIM_INVALID`, `claim.ts`, and the claimant's org made server-side;
  - held names: `nameRefusal`'s `held` argument and `INSTANCE_ORG` (`keel` is an ordinary name);
  - the bootstrap credential: client `keel-bootstrap`, `AgentDirectoryWithBootstrap`, and core's `BOOTSTRAP_CLIENT_ID`, `BOOTSTRAP_AGENT_ID`, `BOOTSTRAP_AGENT`, `PLATFORM_PROJECT_ID` and `isBootstrap`, with enrol's refusal of that identity;

## contracts-v0 (10 Oct 2026)

Changes since `contracts-v0-rc1` (`3e7c7f1`, 10 Oct 2026). Everything is additive or a fix except the `EventOutbox` reshape, which landed before the freeze.

### Added

- `AgentDirectory.verifyCredential(clientId, secret)`: the adapter hashes and compares in constant time, so a client secret never leaves it.
- `declaredEffects?: MFP.Proposition[]` on declaring and revising a change. Advisory only: Keel derives effects from the diff.
- Kernel: pure content hashing (`ContentHash`, `sha256:` + 64 hex) and glob matching.
- Identity: every agent is enrolled in exactly one project. `Agent.projectId`, `Principal.projectId`, the token's `prj` claim, `AgentEnrolled.projectId`, the D1 column `agents.project_id` (migration 0002) and `projectId` on the API's `AgentProfile`.
- Ports `Changes` (the isolate's gateway to the Change DOs: `declare`, `submit`, `get`, `awaitSubmission`) and `Inbox` (`read`, long-polling), each with an in-memory adapter.
- Change RPC `submission(waitMs)`, answering `SubmissionStatus {change, commitSha}`; AgentSession RPC `reserveChange({idempotencyKey, fingerprint})` (a declare retried with the same key reaches the same change; the same key with another body is `IdempotencyConflict`) and `inbox({cursor, waitMs})`.
- Identity domain: `IdempotencyConflict`, and the `AgentNotice` and `InboxPage` schemas (the `Notifier` port re-exports `AgentNotice`, unchanged in shape).
- `CodeHost.diffTrees(repo, base, head)`: the leaves that differ between two commits, as `git diff-tree -r --no-renames` reports them, as `TreeChange` rows in bytewise path order. Unchanged subtrees are skipped by id.
- `CodeHost.delete(repo)`: idempotent. Workspaces are collected by deleting their repo.
- `CodeHost.info(repo)`: `{remote, defaultBranch}`. The remote's host carries the account id, so it goes only to a grant's holder and is never logged.
- `WorkspaceGrant.baseSha` and `WorkspaceGranted.baseSha`: the workspace's head at grant time, which is the change's base. Precheck diffs against it, so a mirror that lags trunk can't leave the base missing.
- WorkspacePool: `grant` is idempotent per holder; a retry returns the same workspace with a freshly minted token. `release(workspaceId, reason?)` takes the `WorkspaceCollected` reason (`landed`, `withdrawn` or `expired`; `withdrawn` when absent). The RPC gains `configure({source, target})` and `status()`.
- `commit()` in the ports: read the clock, then in one `EntityStore` transaction either return what the cause already committed or run the decision and append its events (gap-free `seq` per entity, dedupe keys `<kind>:<id>/<cause>/<type>#<n>`, `notified[]` from the subscription table at commit time). A refusal rolls back every write.
- `EventReceiverRpc.receive(envelopes)`: every Keel Durable Object receives events through this one method; `ChangeRpc`, `AgentSessionRpc` and the other DO contracts extend it. `DeliveryReceipt` and `EventCodecs` in `apps/keel/src/rpc/events.ts`.
- `CodeHostFixture` in `@keeltown/testing`: `commit(repo, ref, files)` and `scratch()`, provided by `makeCodeHostMemory().layer`. Tests seed repos through it, because `CodeHost` never writes git content.

### Fixed

- Scopes are per operation. Each operation now demands only its own scope; the OAuth flow model used to list all four, which overrode the operations and made every operation demand all of them.
- The Change RPC `submit` codec now carries submit's own errors, `ChangeNotFound | LeaseStale | ChangeStateRefused | CommitNotInWorkspace`. It had `RevisionMismatch`, which belongs to revise alone.

### Changed

- `EventOutbox` is reshaped around the outbox fan-out: `append(spec, at, drafts)`, `committed(entity, cause)`, `log(entity)`, `subscribe`, `unsubscribe`, `subscribers(type)` and `receive(dedupeKey, at)`, with its in-memory adapter on the in-memory `EntityStore` so one transaction covers both. It replaces rc1's `append`/`pending`/`acknowledge`.
- The MFP library is pinned at `github:bimsonz/mfp#v0.1.0-pre.3`.
- `CodeHost` reads fail with typed errors instead of dying: `RepoNotFound` (every read, `diffTrees`, `mintToken`, and `fork`'s source), `RefNotFound` (`head`), and `ObjectNotFound` (`readTree`, `readBlob`, and `diffTrees` when the repo lacks `base` or `head`). This narrows the error channels, but the port had no callers yet. Transport failures stay defects. `TreeEntry.type` gains `"commit"` for submodules, and `readTree` is documented as the commit's recursive listing.
- The in-memory `CodeHost` stores real git objects through gitcore. It fails `RepoNotFound` for a repo it never created or has deleted; it used to create the repo on the first read. Its `seed()` is replaced by `CodeHostFixture`.

## contracts-v0-rc1 (10 Oct 2026)

The interface freeze candidate: the TypeSpec spec and its OpenAPI document, the MFP schema v0, every port with an in-memory adapter and contract-suite stub, the use-case signatures, the DO RPC contracts with the Exit envelope, and the runner protocol v0.
